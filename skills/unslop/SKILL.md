# Unslop

Take a text — pasted, attached or a workspace file — and return the same message in a voice that reads written, not generated. Keep the meaning; change the delivery. If the text is for a file, edit it in place or write the revision beside it and say which.

## The tells

- **Throat-clearing**: "Certainly!", "Great question", "In today's fast-paced world", "It's worth noting that", an opening that announces instead of saying.
- **False scaffolding**: "Let's dive in", "In conclusion", "Furthermore/Moreover" chains, numbered "First… Second… Third…" in flowing prose.
- **Hollow emphasis**: "crucial", "vital", "pivotal", "delve", "elevate", "unlock", "game-changer", "seamless", "robust" — adjectives doing the work a fact should do; "realm", "landscape", "tapestry", "a testament to".
- **Tics**: rule-of-three on every point ("simple, powerful, and elegant"), "It's not just X — it's Y", "Whether you're a beginner or an expert", rhetorical questions the text answers itself, an em-dash where a comma or a full stop reads better.
- **Fog**: noun piles where a verb would work, hedges on hedges, a sentence that says the previous one again.

## How to fix

1. Cut before rewriting — most slop deletes cleanly. One sharp sentence beats three smooth ones.
2. Replace the abstract with the specific: numbers, names, the actual thing, over adjectives about the thing.
3. Vary the rhythm on purpose — a short sentence after a long one; not every list has three items.
4. Keep the writer's voice. Remove the tics, not the quirks; formal stays formal, jokes survive.

## Deliver

- Give the revised text, then a few bullets naming the biggest changes and why — not a commentary on every line.
- Flag what you did not fix: claims that need a source, facts that look wrong, anywhere the "slop" might be a deliberate house style.
